TrustCheck Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 10, 2026

TrustCheck protects online shoppers from fraudulent stores, typosquatting, and payment skimming. We are committed to a strict privacy-first architecture: no accounts, no web-browsing tracking, and zero personal data monetization.

Core Promise: TrustCheck does not collect, track, or sell your personal browsing history, passwords, credit card numbers, or personal identifying information.

1. Single Purpose & Data Minimization

TrustCheck has a single purpose: evaluating merchant safety and risk factors on e-commerce websites you visit.

To provide safety ratings without compromising your privacy, TrustCheck isolates only the registrable domain name (such as example.com or store.myshopify.com) using the Public Suffix List (PSL). Full page URLs containing search parameters, affiliate tags, personal tokens, or session IDs are never transmitted to our servers or third-party threat feeds.

2. What Stays Locally on Your Device

TrustCheck operates primarily inside your browser sandbox and stores operational data locally using standard extension storage (chrome.storage.local and chrome.storage.session):

  • User Settings: Your configured options, such as toolbar-only mode, display preferences, and custom backend endpoints.
  • Local Verification Cache: Temporary caching of recently analyzed store domain ratings (up to 6 hours) to prevent redundant lookups.
  • Ephemeral Session Data: Temporary search-page price medians to evaluate discounts on shopping listings within the active browser session.
  • Client Installation Hash: A randomly generated pseudonymous UUID (cryptographically hashed using HMAC) used solely to deduplicate community feedback reports and prevent spam. It is never linked to your identity or hardware.

You can clear this data at any time via the "Reset cache & settings" button in the extension Options page or by uninstalling the extension.

3. Network Connections & Third-Party APIs

When you browse an online store, TrustCheck performs lookup requests to establish domain age and security status:

  • Domain Registry (RDAP): Performs public WHOIS/RDAP queries (via rdap.org or official registries) to verify domain creation dates.
  • Internet Archive (Wayback Machine): Queries the CDX snapshot index (web.archive.org) to establish when a store first appeared online, detecting bought or recycled domains.
  • Google Safe Browsing: If connected, checks whether the site is flagged for known malware, phishing, or unwanted software. Only the origin is checked.
  • Companies House (UK): If a merchant claims a UK registration number in its footer, TrustCheck verifies the public company status against the official UK registry API.
  • TrustCheck Report Cloud: Retrieves aggregated community buyer feedback and verified merchant credentials for the current domain.

4. Developer Disclosures (Web Store Policy Compliance)

In accordance with the Google Chrome Web Store User Data Policy and Mozilla Add-on Policies, we certify that:

  • We do not sell user data to third parties under any circumstances.
  • We do not use or transfer user data for purposes unrelated to the extension's core shopping safety functionality.
  • We do not use or transfer user data to determine creditworthiness or for lending purposes.
  • We do not inject unsolicited advertisements, pop-ups, or search engine redirects into visited web pages.

5. Community Buyer Reports

If you choose to submit feedback on a store (e.g. reporting a successful delivery, delayed order, or missing items):

  • Only the target domain, selected outcome tag, and pseudonymous salted install hash are sent.
  • If an order confirmation was observed by TrustCheck, a proof flag (purchase_observed) is included, but sensitive order details (names, addresses, transaction IDs, payment methods) are completely omitted.
  • All community reports automatically expire and are purged after 90 days.

6. Open Source Transparency & Contact

TrustCheck is open-source software under the Shieldwall project. The entire source code, build scripts, and test suites are publicly inspectable on GitHub.
